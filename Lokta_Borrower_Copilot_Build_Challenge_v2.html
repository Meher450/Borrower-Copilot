Borrower Copilot

Build a personal assistant that helps an Indian borrower answer four questions before they walk into a lender: Should I borrow at all? How much am I really eligible for? What is a fair rate for me? What EMI should I agree to? Then hand them a one-page card they can negotiate with.

Version 1.0Issued 2 Sep 2026Time box 4 daysExpected effort 12 - 16 hours

Why this challenge

Every lender has a model that decides what a borrower gets. The borrower has nothing. They walk in blind, take the first sanction letter, and find out three years later that they paid four points over fair and stretched to 65% of income.

We want to see how you think about that gap. Not a credit model. A self-assessment that makes the borrower the best-informed person in the room.

This is the actual shape of a product we care about. Treat it as real.

What the app must do

A borrower opens the app, answers questions, and gets four outputs. No login, no bureau pull, no personal data stored. Everything runs from what they tell you.

OutputWhat it isWhat good looks like
O1Borrow / Don't borrow / Borrow lessA verdict with a reason. "Don't" is a legitimate answer and must be reachable.
O2Maximum amountTwo numbers, clearly separated: what a lender will likely sanction and what the borrower can safely carry. They are often different. Say which one the borrower should use.
O3Fair interest rateA band, not a point. Show what the borrower should expect and the all-in cost (APR including processing fee), so the lender's quote can be compared honestly.
O4EMI / outflow to agree toA monthly ceiling the borrower should not cross, with the tenure trade-off shown. Include one stress case: income drops or rate rises.

Plus a Negotiation Card. One screen the borrower can hold up to a lender. Lender quotes 14%, card says "fair for your profile is 11 - 12.5%, because...".

The question design

This is the heart of it. Two tiers. You decide the exact questions; below is the intent.

Must questions: the minimum to produce all four outputs

Roughly 8 to 10. If the borrower answers only these, the app still works, with wide ranges and low confidence. Think: purpose, amount wanted, loan type, net monthly income and its type (salaried, self-employed, informal), existing EMIs, household expenses, age, credit score if known.

Additional questions: each one should tighten a range

As many as you think earn their place, but every additional question must change an output. If a question never moves a number, cut it. Think: income stability and history, variable-income share, detail of existing loans, card utilisation, past bounces, emergency savings in months, collateral value, co-applicant, upcoming large expenses, what the loan will earn if it is productive, offers already received.

Rules we will judge you on

  1. Adaptive. A salaried IT employee and a kirana owner should not see the same 30 questions. Skip what does not apply.
  2. Confidence widens with silence. Fewer answers, wider band, and the app says so. Never narrow a range you have no basis to narrow.
  3. Unknown is never zero. "I don't know my credit score" is not a 300. Model it as unknown and show the consequence.
  4. Every number has a why. The borrower must be able to read, in one sentence, why the ceiling is ₹22,000 and not ₹30,000.
  5. India, in rupees. FOIR-style affordability, RBI-style all-in APR disclosure, real product bands (home, LAP, personal, gold, two-wheeler, business). Use what you know; document what you assumed.

Deliverables

  1. The working app. Web or mobile, any stack. Must run locally from your README in under 5 minutes. No backend required.
  2. RULES.md. Every rule, threshold, band and assumption in a table: what · value · why · source or "my judgement". This document is read as carefully as the code.
  3. Three run-throughs. Priya, Ravi and Anita: the questions your app asked each, the four outputs, and the Negotiation Card for each.
  4. A five-minute walkthrough. Screen recording or written. What you would build next, and what you would cut.

Using AI tools to build this is fine and expected. You must be able to defend every rule without them. In the follow-up we will ask you to change a rule live.

How we score

AreaPointsWe are looking for
Domain reasoning30Are the lender's number and the borrower's number correctly different? Does "Don't borrow" fire when it should? Is Ravi routed to a secured product? Is APR honest about fees?
Question design20Tight must-set. Additional questions that each move a number. Adaptive paths. Sensible defaults for the unanswered.
Explainability and the Card20Could a borrower actually use this in a branch? Is every output traceable to answers in one sentence?
Product craft15Flow, copy, ranges shown as ranges, confidence shown honestly, works on a phone.
Engineering10Rules separated from UI. Readable. Runs first time.
Honesty about limits5RULES.md says what you do not know. The app says where it is guessing.

Not scored: pixel perfection, a real bureau integration, a machine-learning model, breadth of loan products beyond what the three borrowers need.
